# Student chama validation kit

**Version:** 1.0  
**Prepared:** 22 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Test whether student savings/contribution groups in Kenya have a recurring, costly administration problem before building or marketing software.

## 1. Recruitment message

### WhatsApp / direct message

Hi! I'm speaking with students who take part in group savings, welfare funds, merry-go-rounds or shared contribution collections. I'm trying to understand how groups currently collect contributions and keep records — I'm not selling anything.

Would you be open to a 15–20 minute conversation? Taking part is completely optional. Please don't share M-Pesa PINs, OTPs, account logins, ID numbers or anyone's private financial details. If you're interested, reply with a time that works and whether you're a group officer or an ordinary member.

### Short version for a group administrator

Hello. I'm conducting a small learning exercise about how student groups manage contributions and shared funds. I'm looking for volunteers for a short 15–20 minute conversation with a group treasurer/organiser and ordinary members. This is research only, not a product launch or sales pitch. Would it be okay to share the invitation with interested members? No member contact details should be shared with me without their permission.

## 2. Participant information and consent

Read this before the interview:

I'm trying to understand how student groups currently manage contributions, savings, welfare funds or shared collections. The conversation should take about 15–20 minutes. There is no product to buy and no promise of a future benefit.

Taking part is voluntary. You can skip any question or stop at any time. I'll use a group code and participant role rather than your name in the research notes. I will not ask for M-Pesa PINs, OTPs, passwords, ID documents, account numbers or exact personal balances. Please avoid sharing other members' personal information.

I would like to take written notes. I will only make an audio recording if you separately agree. Any screenshots or documents must be optional, relevant and redacted before I keep them. The findings may be summarised without identifying you or your group.

**Consent checks**
- [ ] Participant understands the purpose and voluntary nature of the conversation.
- [ ] Participant agrees to take part.
- [ ] Participant agrees to written notes.
- [ ] Participant separately agrees to audio recording, if recording is genuinely needed.
- [ ] Participant agrees to show a redacted workflow artefact, if relevant.

Do not record if consent is not explicit. Do not treat silence as consent.

## 3. Quick eligibility screener

Ask only what is needed to route the conversation:
1. Are you currently a student in a Kenyan college, TVET institution or university?
2. Have you personally participated in a student group that collected contributions, pooled savings, ran a welfare fund or rotated payouts during the last six months?
3. What kind of group was it? (Savings / merry-go-round / welfare / investment / shared class or club collection / other)
4. What was your role during the most recent cycle? (Treasurer/organiser / officer / ordinary member)
5. Would you be comfortable describing the last cycle without sharing private account or member details?

If the person has no recent experience, thank them and do not count the conversation as evidence of a current group workflow.

## 4. Interview script

Do not introduce a proposed app or describe desired features before the behaviour questions. Ask for the most recent real example.

1. **Recent cycle:** "Walk me through the most recent contribution cycle, from the first reminder to closing the records."
2. **Recording:** "How were payments made and recorded? What happened after someone paid?"
3. **Reconciliation:** "How did you confirm that the records matched the money received? Who did that work, and how long did it take?"
4. **Failure:** "Tell me about the last late payment, missing record, mismatch or disagreement. What happened next?"
5. **Tools:** "Which tools did the group actually use — M-Pesa, WhatsApp, a spreadsheet, a notebook, a bank statement or an app?"
6. **Workaround:** "What did the group try before the current method? Why did it keep or abandon that approach?"
7. **Decision-making:** "Who can change the process or approve a tool? Does the group have a budget for administration?"
8. **Refusal:** "What would make the group refuse a digital tool, even if it were free?"

Useful neutral follow-ups:
- "Can you give me a recent example?"
- "When did that last happen?"
- "How often has it happened in the last three cycles?"
- "What did you do immediately after that?"
- "Who else was involved?"
- "What makes the current method acceptable despite that difficulty?"

Avoid leading questions such as "Would an app that automatically tracks M-Pesa help?" or "Would you pay KES 100?" until actual behaviour and existing spend have been discussed.

## 5. Optional workflow observation

Only observe with the participant's permission. Ask them to demonstrate the process using a redacted or dummy example. Do not take custody of a phone, account or financial records.

Observe:
- How a contribution becomes due.
- How the group reminds members.
- How payment is confirmed.
- How a cash contribution is handled.
- How records are updated.
- How a mismatch or late payment is resolved.
- How members view their own records.
- How a cycle is closed and the next rotation/period is decided.

Do not photograph or retain:
- M-Pesa messages containing names, phone numbers, transaction codes or balances.
- Bank statements or account screens with identifiers.
- National ID cards or student ID cards.
- Private WhatsApp messages unrelated to the workflow.
- Member lists containing unnecessary personal information.

If an artefact is useful, ask the participant to create a redacted copy with fictitious names and masked transaction references. If that is not possible, record a description instead of retaining the artefact.

### Evidence labels

- **REPORTED:** participant describes what happened; not independently observed.
- **OBSERVED:** researcher directly sees the workflow being performed.
- **DOCUMENTED:** a redacted, consented artefact supports the statement.
- **INFERENCE:** researcher interpretation based on evidence.
- **UNKNOWN:** not established.

Do not turn a participant's opinion into a verified fact. Preserve contradictory evidence.

## 8. Analysis worksheet

For each recurring problem, record:
- How many independent groups described it?
- How many participants described a recent example?
- How recently did it occur?
- How often does it happen?
- What is the actual consequence?
- What does the group currently do instead?
- Is there a free or low-cost competitor that already solves it?
- Who has authority and budget to change the process?
- What evidence contradicts the proposed opportunity?

Separate three questions:
1. Does the group have a real problem?
2. Does the proposed workflow solve that problem better than its current method and alternatives?
3. Will the group adopt and pay for it?

A positive answer to one does not prove the others.

## 9. Proposed decision thresholds

These are experiment rules for the founder, not external market benchmarks.

**Continue problem validation** if at least 8 of 12 participants describe a recent, concrete problem and at least 4 independent groups show a similar recurring workflow issue.

**Prototype one narrow workflow** only if at least 3 groups agree to test it during a real upcoming cycle, and officers and ordinary members can explain the value without prompting.

**Test willingness to pay** separately. Seek at least 2 real, clearly disclosed paid-pilot commitments from groups with authority to approve payment. Verbal enthusiasm is not purchase evidence.

**Stop or pivot** if the current workaround is good enough, the problem is rare, the free competitors solve it, members distrust shared records, officers cannot change the process, or no realistic budget owner exists.

## 11. Safety, privacy and conduct

- Do not collect, hold, transfer or lend group money during validation.
- Do not request passwords, M-Pesa PINs, OTPs, account access or identity documents.
- Do not collect exact balances or transaction identifiers unless a separately reviewed, consented and redacted research need exists; the default is not to collect them.
- Do not add participants to marketing lists without separate permission.
- Do not publish quotes, screenshots or group names that could identify a participant.
- Store notes in a private, access-controlled location. Share anonymised summaries only.
- Set a deletion date for raw notes and redacted artefacts after the study.
- Do not use AI to process identifiable notes. Remove names, phone numbers, institution-specific identifiers and account details first.
- Do not send unsolicited bulk messages, scrape class groups or imply institutional endorsement.
- Agents may draft materials and analyse anonymised notes, but a human must approve outreach and any publication.
